# Systems Thinking in Green Building

## Definition

Systems thinking treats a building as a changing network of materials, resources, energy, people, information, and relationships across space and time. A decision is evaluated by how it changes the wider system, not only by its local effect.

## Sourced Facts

- The guide describes the built environment as a series of relationships in which each part can affect many other parts. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 026|PDF p. 26; guide p. 19]]
- It contrasts open systems that import resources and export waste with more closed systems that slow resource throughput and make outputs useful elsewhere. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 028|PDF p. 28; guide p. 21]]
- Feedback loops, information flows, delays, goals, and mindsets can all be leverage points for changing system behavior. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 029|PDF p. 29; guide p. 22]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 030|PDF p. 30; guide p. 23]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 031|PDF p. 31; guide p. 24]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 032|PDF p. 32; guide p. 25]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 033|PDF p. 33; guide p. 26]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 034|PDF p. 34; guide p. 27]]

## Interpretation

> [!note] Interpretation
> For design and operations, define the system boundary explicitly, map inputs and outputs, identify delayed effects, and look for interventions that improve several outcomes at once. A local efficiency gain is not automatically a system improvement if it shifts harm to another life-cycle stage or building subsystem.
