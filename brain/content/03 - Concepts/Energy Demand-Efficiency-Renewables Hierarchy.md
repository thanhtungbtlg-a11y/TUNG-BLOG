# Energy Demand-Efficiency-Renewables Hierarchy

## Definition

The energy hierarchy is to reduce loads first, meet those loads efficiently second, supply the remaining demand with lower-impact renewable energy third, and continuously verify actual performance.

## Sourced Facts

- The guide begins with conservation and demand reduction, including passive use of daylight, thermal mass, natural ventilation, solar orientation, and occupant feedback. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 072|PDF p. 72; guide p. 65]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 073|PDF p. 73; guide p. 66]]
- After demand reduction, efficiency means using less energy for the same work; envelope improvements may reduce required HVAC capacity through system synergy. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 074|PDF p. 74; guide p. 67]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 075|PDF p. 75; guide p. 68]]
- Remaining demand can be addressed through on-site generation or off-site renewable-energy mechanisms described by the guide. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 076|PDF p. 76; guide p. 69]]
- Monitoring, commissioning, owner requirements, staff training, maintenance, and occupant engagement are needed to sustain performance. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 076|PDF p. 76; guide p. 69]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 077|PDF p. 77; guide p. 70]]

## Interpretation

> [!note] Interpretation
> Size active systems only after passive and load-reduction measures are represented. Keep the predicted-to-measured feedback loop active after occupancy so design efficiency does not remain merely modeled efficiency.
