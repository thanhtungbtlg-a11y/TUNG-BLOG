# Integrative Process for Building Performance

## Definition

An integrative process brings owners, designers, engineers, constructors, operators, occupants, and other stakeholders together early to identify interactions and synergies among building and site systems.

## Sourced Facts

- The guide defines the process as a comprehensive approach in which the project team coordinates building systems and equipment to improve performance, comfort, and environmental outcomes. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 037|PDF p. 37; guide p. 30]]
- It identifies three connected phases: discovery; design and construction; and occupancy, operations, and performance feedback. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 037|PDF p. 37; guide p. 30]]
- Early discovery is emphasized because owner aspirations, performance goals, project needs, and system interactions can be addressed before later design changes become costly. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 037|PDF p. 37; guide p. 30]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 038|PDF p. 38; guide p. 31]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 039|PDF p. 39; guide p. 32]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 040|PDF p. 40; guide p. 33]]

## Interpretation

> [!note] Interpretation
> Use shared performance targets before selecting systems. Test combinations across disciplines, document assumptions and trade-offs, and keep operators involved so design intent can survive commissioning and occupancy.
