# Adaptive Reuse and Future Adaptability

## Definition

Adaptive reuse redesigns an existing structure for a use significantly different from its original purpose. Future adaptability designs a project so changing needs can be accommodated without premature obsolescence. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 019|PDF page 19]]

## Why it matters

Reusing a structure can avoid impacts from new material extraction and demolition waste, retain an already serviced site, and reduce pressure to convert farmland or forest. Flexible design can extend those benefits by supporting later changes in use. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 019|PDF page 19]]

## How it works

Teams evaluate whether an existing building can serve a new function and whether new work can accommodate evolving uses. The guide illustrates adaptability with a floor plan capable of changing from offices to apartments and with transport networks that support multiple travel modes. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 019|PDF page 19]]

## LEED relevance

The source presents reuse and adaptability as sustainability strategies. It does not state current LEED reuse calculations or thresholds on this page. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 019|PDF page 19]]

## Application to MEP and piping engineering

> [!note] Practical engineering interpretation
> This section is an engineering interpretation, not a LEED requirement stated by the source.

- Preserve usable systems and distribution routes when their condition and future service are verified.
- Allow practical access, isolation, capacity review, and reconfiguration where foreseeable changes in use justify them.
