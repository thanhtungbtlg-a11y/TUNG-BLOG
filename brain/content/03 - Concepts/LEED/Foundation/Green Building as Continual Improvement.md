# Green Building as Continual Improvement

## Definition

The guide defines green building as a process in which current best practices become standard practices and create a higher starting point for future performance. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 010|PDF page 10]]

## Why it matters

Building-system performance can degrade over time. Without maintenance and feedback, a project that performs well initially may not preserve that performance through its life. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 018|PDF page 18]]

## How it works

Continual improvement connects design for future conditions, operator participation, monitoring, maintenance, verification, and corrective action. The source describes operators as participants who need information to monitor and maintain performance. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 018|PDF page 18]]

## LEED relevance

The guide associates green building with a permanent shift in design, planning, construction, and operations practices. This principle explains the intended direction of improvement without specifying a current LEED threshold. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 010|PDF page 10]]

## Application to MEP and piping engineering

> [!note] Practical engineering interpretation
> This section is an engineering interpretation, not a LEED requirement stated by the source.

- Pair design targets with trendable measurements, maintenance responsibilities, and a corrective-action path.
- Preserve design assumptions and operating information so later teams can improve performance rather than rediscover intent.
