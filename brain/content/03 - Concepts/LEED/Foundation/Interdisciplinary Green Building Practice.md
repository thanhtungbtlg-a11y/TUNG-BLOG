# Interdisciplinary Green Building Practice

## Definition

Interdisciplinary green-building practice is collaborative, iterative work in which people from different disciplines question assumptions, share knowledge, and develop solutions together rather than passing isolated work down a linear sequence. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 023|PDF page 23]]

## Why it matters

The guide states that green building requires new skills, knowledge, attitudes, and mindsets. It emphasizes teamwork, critical thinking, productive discussion, facilitation, and the ability to engage with expertise outside one's own professional background. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 023|PDF page 23]]

## How it works

Participants ask why customary approaches are used, explore alternatives together, and build feedback loops into the process. Learning occurs through cross-disciplinary interaction, experience, workshops, classes, and other training. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 023|PDF page 23]]

## LEED relevance

The guide says progress beyond checklist use requires a new approach to creating and caring for the built environment. This process concept does not prescribe current LEED team roles or credentials. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 023|PDF page 23]]

## Application to MEP and piping engineering

> [!note] Practical engineering interpretation
> This section is an engineering interpretation, not a LEED requirement stated by the source.

- Bring MEP assumptions into shared discussions early enough for architecture, structure, site, operations, and cost decisions to respond.
- Ask questions in a form other disciplines can test, and document decisions that depend on cross-system coordination.
