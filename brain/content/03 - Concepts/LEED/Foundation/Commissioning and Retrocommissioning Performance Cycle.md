# Commissioning and Retrocommissioning Performance Cycle

## Definition

Commissioning verifies and documents that a building, its systems, and its assemblies are planned, designed, installed, tested, operated, and maintained to meet the owner's project requirements. Retrocommissioning is a later tune-up that identifies inefficiencies and restores performance. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 018|PDF page 18]]

## Why it matters

The guide explains that even strong initial performance can decline as systems degrade. Commissioning supports the initial outcome, while retrocommissioning and monitoring address performance loss during operations. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 018|PDF page 18]]

## How it works

The cycle begins with owner requirements, continues through design and installation verification, and extends into operation, maintenance, monitoring, and later corrective review. Operators need participation and information to sustain that cycle. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 018|PDF page 18]]

## LEED relevance

The guide identifies commissioning and retrocommissioning as green-building performance practices and says they are reviewed later in its application-strategy section. This note does not state current LEED scope, frequency, or documentation requirements. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 018|PDF page 18]]

## Application to MEP and piping engineering

> [!note] Practical engineering interpretation
> This section is an engineering interpretation, not a LEED requirement stated by the source.

- Translate owner requirements into verifiable sequences, setpoints, tests, trend points, and acceptance evidence.
- Plan how operators will detect drift and initiate corrective review after initial commissioning is complete.
