# Climate Resilience for Green Projects

## Definition

Climate resilience is the capacity of a project to anticipate likely future stressors, adapt to changing conditions, and continue serving its purpose over time. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 017|PDF page 17]]

## Why it matters

The guide states that reducing climate impacts is not enough; buildings and communities also need preparation for climate consequences. It identifies higher temperatures, drought, flooding, and sea-level change as threats whose regional effects will vary. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 015|PDF page 15]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 017|PDF page 17]]

## How it works

Teams assess likely local threats and select adaptive responses. The source examples connect shrinking snowpack with conservation, storage, and alternative water sources, and connect hotter summers with cooling capacity and air-quality concerns. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 018|PDF page 18]]

## LEED relevance

The guide frames flexibility, adaptability, and resilience as attributes of green projects. These pages do not provide a current LEED resilience credit or threshold. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 017|PDF page 17]]

## Application to MEP and piping engineering

> [!note] Practical engineering interpretation
> This section is an engineering interpretation, not a LEED requirement stated by the source.

- Document future-condition assumptions separately from present design conditions.
- Test whether cooling, ventilation, drainage, storage, controls, and water-source strategies remain workable under the selected stress scenarios.
