# Green Building Market Transformation

## Definition

Market transformation is the guide's stated goal of fundamentally changing how buildings and communities are designed, built, and operated. It describes certification as a way to recognize levels of achievement and help shift prevailing practice. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 014|PDF page 14]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 015|PDF page 15]]

## Why it matters

The source describes a reinforcing change in which demand for green buildings, products, technologies, incentives, and skilled professionals can make green practices increasingly mainstream. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 014|PDF page 14]]

## How it works

The guide attributes market change to education, advocacy, research, local networks, certification, public policy, financial incentives, and expanding products and professional capability. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 014|PDF page 14]]

## LEED relevance

The guide identifies LEED as USGBC's green-building program and explicitly connects its certification role to market transformation. These statements describe the source edition and do not supply current rating-system rules.
