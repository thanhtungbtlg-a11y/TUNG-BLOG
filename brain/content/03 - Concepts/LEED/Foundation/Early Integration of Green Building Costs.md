# Early Integration of Green Building Costs

## Definition

Early cost integration treats sustainability as part of a project's functional requirements and system design rather than as a late add-on to a minimally compliant baseline. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 021|PDF page 21]]

## Why it matters

The guide states that long-term savings can offset some initial expenditures and that the best returns are realized when green building enters the process early. Late treatment can hide interactions among scope, performance, and cost. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 021|PDF page 21]]

## How it works

Teams evaluate system interactions before fixing component sizes and budgets. The source illustrates this with higher-performance windows that may allow a smaller and less costly HVAC system. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 021|PDF page 21]]

## LEED relevance

The source discusses cost integration as a green-building process principle and cites historical cost research. It does not establish a LEED cost prerequisite, premium, or payback rule. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 021|PDF page 21]]

## Application to MEP and piping engineering

> [!note] Practical engineering interpretation
> This section is an engineering interpretation, not a LEED requirement stated by the source.

- Compare packages of coordinated measures rather than pricing each MEP component independently.
- Recalculate loads, equipment capacity, distribution, controls, and operating effects when envelope or use assumptions change.
