# Materials Life-Cycle and Waste Hierarchy

## Definition

Material decisions should minimize demand and embodied impacts across the full life cycle, with prevention and reuse considered before recycling or downstream waste treatment.

## Sourced Facts

- The guide connects material impacts to extraction, production, transportation, consumption, and disposal. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 078|PDF p. 78; guide p. 71]]
- It identifies reuse of existing buildings and salvaged materials, compact and flexible design, efficient framing, and operational source reduction as conservation strategies. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 078|PDF p. 78; guide p. 71]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 079|PDF p. 79; guide p. 72]]
- Product transparency tools such as life-cycle assessment, environmental product declarations, and ingredient disclosures can support comparison and reduce reliance on unsupported green claims. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 080|PDF p. 80; guide p. 73]]
- The waste hierarchy presented in the guide ranks source reduction, reuse, recycling, and waste-to-energy as preferred strategies for reducing landfill disposal, in that order. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 081|PDF p. 81; guide p. 74]]

## Interpretation

> [!note] Interpretation
> Begin procurement with the question of whether new material is needed. When it is, compare verified life-cycle information and service performance; then plan for efficient installation, future adaptability, recovery, and documented waste handling.
