# Life-Cycle Approach to Building Decisions

## Definition

A life-cycle approach compares alternatives across extraction, manufacture, transport, installation, use, maintenance, adaptation, and end of life so that upstream or downstream impacts are not hidden by a single attractive attribute.

## Sourced Facts

- The guide states that building life-cycle stages include design, construction, operations and maintenance, refurbishment, renovation, and ultimately demolition or preferably reuse. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 034|PDF p. 34; guide p. 27]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 035|PDF p. 35; guide p. 28]]
- Embodied energy includes energy used to harvest or extract, manufacture, transport, install, and use a product across its life cycle. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 035|PDF p. 35; guide p. 28]]
- Life-cycle assessment evaluates environmental effects, while life-cycle costing evaluates costs; the methods use different data and answer different questions. [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 035|PDF p. 35; guide p. 28]] [[02 - Sources/PDF/leed-coreconcepts-strategies-3rd-edition/pages/Page 036|PDF p. 36; guide p. 29]]

## Interpretation

> [!note] Interpretation
> Compare complete service outcomes rather than isolated product labels. Include durability, maintainability, adaptability, operating performance, replacement cycles, and end-of-life pathways alongside first cost and embodied impacts.
