|Provide closed risers (visually and<br>physically) on all stairs.|Closed risers eliminate gaps, reduce the risk of tripping or<br>falling, particularly for small children or pets, and make<br>stairs more secure. |Use visual contrast between walls and<br>floors, walls and doors, and walls and<br>casework.|Incorporating distinct visual contrast between different<br>architectural elements improves navigation and wayfinding,<br>particularly for individuals with visual impairments. Projects<br>must use contrasting colors, textures, and/or material for<br>walls, floors, doors, casework i.e., drawers and cabinet<br>doors, or other permanently installed features.|
|Provide visual, tactile, contrasting, or<br>photoluminescent warnings at floor<br>level changes.|Incorporate bold color contrasts or reflective strips to mark<br>transitions, use textured materials such as ribbed or<br>grooved strips to provide tactile cues, and apply varied<br>surface finishes to create both visual and tactile distinctions.<br>Photoluminescent markings are recommended to be applied<br>to ensure visibility in low-light conditions, particularly in<br>staircases or emergency exit areas.|

Use<br>a height-adjustable adult changing table with a weight<br>capacity of at least 350 lbs (160 kg) to accommodate<br>diverse user needs. Ensure the table is at least 72 inches|
